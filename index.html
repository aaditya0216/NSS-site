About

What is NSS?

The National Service Scheme is a Central Government programme run under the Ministry of Youth Affairs and Sports. It was launched on 24 September 1969, the birth centenary year of Mahatma Gandhi, in 37 universities with about 40,000 students.

Its main aim is the personality development of students through community service. Volunteers come from colleges, universities and senior secondary schools.

Motto

"Not Me, But You"

The motto reflects democratic living and selfless service. It teaches volunteers to respect other people's views and care for fellow beings, and it rests on the belief that an individual's welfare depends on the welfare of society as a whole.

Symbol

The Konark Wheel

The NSS badge is based on the giant Rath wheel of the Konark Sun Temple in Odisha. The wheel shows the cycle of creation, preservation and release, and stands for continuous change and striving for social transformation.

  • The 8 bars stand for the 24 hours of the day, so volunteers are ready to serve round the clock
  • Red shows energy and high spirit
  • Navy blue stands for the cosmos
  • White stands for purity

What volunteers do

Activities

Each volunteer completes regular activities of 240 hours and takes part in a 7-day special camp, often in an adopted village.

  • Environment care and tree plantation
  • Health, hygiene and blood donation drives
  • Literacy and awareness programmes
  • Disaster relief and rural development
Objectives

Why NSS matters

  • Understand the community and its problems
  • Build leadership and teamwork
  • Develop civic responsibility and patriotism
  • Promote national integration and social harmony
